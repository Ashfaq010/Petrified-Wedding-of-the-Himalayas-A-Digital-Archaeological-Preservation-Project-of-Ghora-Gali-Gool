Petrified Wedding
of the Himalayas

Ghora Gali Gool  ·  Indian Occupied Kashmir

Abstract

This project digitally documents Ghora Gali, an archaeological site in the Himalayan mountains of Indian Occupied Kashmir featuring around 200 stone sculptures. Despite its historical and cultural significance, Ghora Gali remains largely unrecorded and at risk of erasure. Using photography, videography, GPS geotagging, and 3D modeling, this project creates an online archive preserving Ghora Gali's material history and local folklore.

Growing up, I was told never to go to Ghora Gali.

Ghora Gali — horse alley — Gool in Indian Occupied Kashmir is considered off limits, shrouded in local cautionary tales and folklore. My mother, like many others in my village who could not read or write but carried vast archives in their memories, would lower her voice whenever the name Ghora Gali came up.

Folklore goes that in the Himalayan forests, long long ago, a wedding procession climbed the mountain pass: men, women and children on horses, drums echoing across the Pir Panjal range. Before they could reach their destination, the jinns — demons and spirits — arrived and overpowered the wedding party, turning everyone into stone. The wedding froze in time. The jinn never left.

"At Ghora Gali, history did not end. It hardened and waited."

Only later did I learn that Ghora Gali is one of the most significant archaeological sites in India, with around two hundred unique stone sculptures of mounted horsemen, referred to as the Horsemen of the Pir Panjal, Kashmir's Lost Hun Riders, or simply the Petrified Wedding.

The goals of this project are:

  • To digitally preserve Ghora Gali and the folktales associated with it.
  • To make this archive freely available online for scholars worldwide.
  • To petition the Archaeological Survey of India and UNESCO to declare Ghora Gali a World Heritage Site.

Methodology

How This Project Works

Click each section below to expand it and read about the methodology.

A. Digital Archaeology and 3D Modeling ▼
I plan to photograph, videograph, and geotag all 200 stone sculptures at Ghora Gali during a three-week visit in March 2026. My ultimate ambition is to use high-definition 2D photographs to create 3D models of each sculpture type using SAM 3D or Copilot 3D — both free and open tools. These models will be available in open formats enabling reuse across platforms and long-term preservation.
B. Static Digital Publishing ▼
I will publish the project as a static website built with Jekyll and hosted on GitHub Pages. Static websites are lightweight, stable, and sustainable. The website will have pages divided into sculpture categories, with each page displaying 2D photographs and a 3D model embedded via Google Model Viewer, which supports open access without proprietary lock-in.
C. Structured Metadata and Reusability ▼
Each documented sculpture will be accompanied by structured metadata including a unique identifier, material and condition notes, iconographic description, stylistic and comparative notes such as Central Asian or Indo-Greek parallels, and sculpture category. This transforms the project from a descriptive website into a reusable scholarly resource.
D. Critical Scholarly Paper ▼
Alongside technical documentation, the project will lead to a scholarly paper addressing the politics of archaeological neglect in Kashmir, bureaucratic regimes of preservation and abandonment, and digital heritage as counter-infrastructure. Digital preservation becomes an ethical and scholarly intervention, producing counter-infrastructures that resist disappearance by stabilizing endangered material culture in accessible form.
Location

Gool, Ramban District, Jammu and Kashmir

Ghora Gali is situated in the Pir Panjal range, in the Gool area of Ramban district, Indian administered Jammu and Kashmir — one of the most militarized and least accessible zones on earth.

Project Timeline

Phases of the Work

  • March 2026
    Three-week field visit to Ghora Gali. High-definition photography and videography of all 200 stone sculptures. GPS geotagging and preliminary database creation.
  • June 2026
    Tagging, categorization, and metadata creation. Generation of 3D models using SAM 3D, categorized by size, shape, number of figures, weapons, gender, and narrative.
  • July 2026
    Building and hosting the full archive website with Jekyll on GitHub Pages. Embedding 3D models using Google Model Viewer.
  • August 2026
    Scholarly paper submitted to South Asian and global academic conferences on digital heritage and archaeology.
  • September 2026 and beyond
    Formal petition to the Archaeological Survey of India and UNESCO to preserve Ghora Gali and declare it a World Heritage Site.
